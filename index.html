The premise

One trip. Three problems worth solving properly.

Most travel apps try to be the whole holiday and end up thin everywhere. Minato Studio does the opposite: three small apps, each doing one hard thing completely.

Where did the money go?

Four wallets, three currencies, and a pile of receipts nobody wants to sort at midnight. That's TabiLog.

Can everyone survive this day?

A day that suits a 38-year-old can quietly break a 3-year-old and a 78-year-old. That's TabiPace.

What are we doing on day nine?

The whole trip on one screen, dense enough to replace the spreadsheet. That's TabiPlanner.

How these are built

Four rules, held on purpose

Apps in this category rarely die of missing features. They die of data loss, sync bugs and slow decay. So the rules are about durability, not ambition.

  1. Offline is the default, not the upgrade

    These get used in rural Japan with no signal. Nothing important should wait for a network.

  2. Your data leaves whenever you want

    CSV, Excel and PDF export from day one. No format you can't open somewhere else.

  3. Collect as little as possible

    Your data is never sold or handed to a data broker. TabiPace goes furthest: no server, no account, nothing leaves the device. TabiLog's Android build carries ads, and the privacy page says exactly what that means.

  4. Finished beats feature-rich

    A small app that works every time is worth more than a large one that mostly does.
